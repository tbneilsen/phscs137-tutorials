Geoengineering: Can We Deliberately Change Earth's Climate?

PHSCS 137 · Interactive tutorial · About 20 minutes

1. What is climate engineering?

Climate engineering (also called geoengineering) refers to deliberate, large-scale interventions in Earth's climate system intended to limit global warming or its effects. The ideas in this tutorial use one of two broad strategies:

1. Modify Earth's energy balance: Reflect more incoming sunlight back to space or allow more outgoing infrared radiation to escape. These approaches are often grouped under solar radiation modification (SRM), although cirrus cloud thinning works by changing outgoing infrared radiation rather than reflecting sunlight.

2. Remove carbon dioxide from the atmosphere: Carbon dioxide removal (CDR) takes CO₂ out of the atmosphere and aims to store the carbon for a long time. Ocean seeding is one proposed approach, but whether it results in durable carbon removal is uncertain.

Both strategies seek to influence climate, but only carbon removal directly reduces atmospheric CO₂. The six ideas below illustrate different mechanisms and tradeoffs.

Geoengineering is not a substitute for reducing greenhouse-gas emissions. These proposals have different levels of scientific evidence, practicality, cost, and potential side effects; most are not proven safe or effective at climate-relevant scale.

3. How does reflecting sunlight change Earth's energy balance?

At the top of the atmosphere, Earth's globally averaged absorbed sunlight is (1 − α)S/4, where α is planetary albedo and S is the solar flux at Earth's orbit. Explore a hypothetical intervention that reduces sunlight reaching Earth or increases its reflectivity.

4. What can sunlight reflection NOT directly fix?

Solar radiation modification may counter some warming, but it does not remove excess atmospheric CO₂. For example, ocean acidification from dissolved CO₂ would continue unless atmospheric CO₂ is reduced.
