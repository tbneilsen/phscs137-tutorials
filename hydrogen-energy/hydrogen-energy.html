Hydrogen Energy: From Water to Fuel and Back

PHSCS 137 · Interactive tutorial · Approximately 15–20 minutes

1. What is electrolysis?

Electrolysis uses electricity to split water into hydrogen and oxygen. (Hydrolysis is a broader term for chemical reactions involving water; water electrolysis is the process used here.) An electrolyzer needs an external source of electrical energy.

2 H₂O (liquid) + electrical energy → 2 H₂ (gas) + O₂ (gas)

Follow the process

  1. Electricity supplies the energy needed to drive the chemical reaction.
  2. Water molecules are split through reactions at the electrodes.
  3. Hydrogen gas (H₂) is collected on one side; oxygen gas (O₂) on the other.
  4. Hydrogen may be compressed, stored, transported, and later used as fuel.

Hydrogen is an energy carrier, not a primary energy source. Producing hydrogen requires energy from somewhere else, such as wind, solar, nuclear, or fossil fuels.

2. How much gas is produced?

For every two water molecules consumed, electrolysis makes two H₂ molecules and one O₂ molecule. The ratio of hydrogen to oxygen molecules is 2:1.

Water molecules8 H₂O
Hydrogen molecules8 H₂
Oxygen molecules4 O₂

This illustrates chemical stoichiometry, not the actual production rate of an electrolyzer.

3. Convert electrical energy into stored hydrogen energy

Change the electrical power supplied to an electrolyzer, operating time, and energy-conversion efficiency. The model estimates the chemical energy stored in hydrogen and the mass produced.

Electrical energy input800 kWh
Hydrogen chemical energy560 kWh
Hydrogen produced16.8 kg
Conversion losses240 kWh

Electrical energy input (kWh) = input power (kW) × time (h)

Hydrogen energy stored (kWh) = electrical energy input (kWh) × efficiency

Hydrogen mass (kg) = stored hydrogen energy (kWh) ÷ 33.3 kWh/kg

We use the lower heating value (LHV) of hydrogen, approximately 33.3 kWh/kg. The efficiency slider is defined on this same LHV basis. Actual equipment performance varies, and additional energy may be needed for water treatment, compression, and storage.

4. Turn stored hydrogen back into electricity

In a hydrogen fuel cell, hydrogen reacts electrochemically with oxygen from the air. The cell produces electricity, water, and heat. Unlike combustion in an engine, the chemical reaction can directly produce electrical energy. Electrolysis is the first energy conversion (electricity into hydrogen chemical energy); the fuel cell is the second (hydrogen chemical energy back into electricity). Hydrogen is stored in the tank between these two steps.

2 H₂ + O₂ → 2 H₂O + electrical energy + heat

5. How much electricity can you get back?

The hydrogen stored after electrolysis (the first conversion) becomes the fuel for the fuel cell (the second conversion). Adjust the fuel-cell efficiency to see how much of that stored chemical energy is recovered as electricity.

Initial electrical input800 kWh
Hydrogen energy stored560 kWh
Electricity recovered308 kWh
Round-trip efficiency38.5%
Total energy not recovered as electricity492 kWh

Recovered electricity = hydrogen energy × fuel-cell efficiency

Round-trip efficiency = electrolyzer efficiency × fuel-cell efficiency

Hydrogen can be valuable for long-duration energy storage and some industrial uses, but the electricity → hydrogen → electricity pathway has substantial conversion losses. Compression, transport, and other auxiliary losses would reduce the round-trip efficiency further.
