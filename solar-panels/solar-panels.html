Solar Photovoltaics: How Sunlight Becomes Electricity

1. How does a photovoltaic cell work?

A photovoltaic (PV) cell is a semiconductor device, commonly made of silicon. A photon of sunlight can transfer energy to an electron in the semiconductor. If that photon has sufficient energy, it creates a mobile electron and a corresponding hole. The built-in electric field near a p–n junction helps separate the charges. When a circuit is connected, the charges can move through the circuit and deliver electrical power.

Follow the energy

  1. Sunlight delivers energy to the semiconductor.
  2. Absorbed photons can create mobile charge carriers.
  3. The junction's electric field helps separate electrons and holes.
  4. With a connected circuit, the cell delivers direct-current (DC) electrical power.

Important: A PV cell does not store the sunlight as electricity. It converts incoming light energy into electrical energy while illuminated. A separate battery is needed to store energy for later use.

Incident solar power (W) = sunlight intensity (W/m²) × panel area (m²)

Electrical power output (W) = incident solar power (W) × efficiency

P = (1000 W/m²) × (1.5 m²) × 0.20 = 300 W

Efficiency must be written as a fraction in the calculation (for example, 20% = 0.20). This simplified model assumes perpendicular sunlight and constant efficiency. In reality, efficiency also depends on temperature and other operating conditions.

3. Why does orientation matter?

For the same sunlight intensity, a panel receives the most power when sunlight arrives perpendicular to its surface. Tilting a panel away from the Sun spreads the same beam across a larger area, reducing the power intercepted per square meter of panel.

Angle of incidence is measured between incoming sunlight and the line perpendicular (normal) to the panel.

Intercepted sunlight ∝ cos(θ)

θ = 0° means sunlight strikes perpendicular to the panel. θ = 90° means sunlight runs parallel to its surface. This is the same geometry as using sin(A) when A is measured from the panel surface.
