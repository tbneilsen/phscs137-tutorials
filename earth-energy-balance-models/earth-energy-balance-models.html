Earth's Energy Balance: Sunlight In, Infrared Out

1. Think of Earth as a bank account

Deposits add money; withdrawals remove it. Likewise, absorbed sunlight adds energy to Earth and outgoing infrared radiation removes energy to space. A balance that grows means energy is accumulating.

Starting balance$1,000
Daily net change$0
Ending balance$1,000

Change in stored amount = (input rate − output rate) × time

Connection to Earth: When absorbed solar power exceeds outgoing infrared power, Earth gains energy. When they are equal, Earth's total stored energy is steady. A climate system can still exchange large amounts of energy while in balance.

2. Zeroth-order model — Earth without an infrared-absorbing atmosphere

Solar flux is the rate at which solar energy passes through a unit area, measured in W/m². Here, the solar constant S is the solar flux arriving at Earth’s distance from the Sun on a surface perpendicular to the sunlight. Albedo (α) is the fraction of incoming sunlight reflected back to space rather than absorbed. Earth intercepts sunlight over a circular cross section (πR²) but radiates over its entire spherical surface (4πR²), producing the factor of 1/4 in the global average.

Mean incoming sunlight340 W/m²
Absorbed sunlight238 W/m²
Equilibrium temperature255 K

Absorbed solar flux = (1 − α) S / 4 = σ T⁴

T = [(1 − α) S / (4σ)]1/4

σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W/(m²·K⁴); α is albedo. Emissivity is assumed to be 1. The calculated temperature is Earth's effective radiating temperature, not its actual average surface temperature.

3. First-order model — add an atmospheric layer

This model adds an atmosphere above the surface. Greenhouse gases absorb some outgoing infrared radiation and re-emit it in all directions, including downward toward the surface. The atmosphere is treated as transparent to incoming sunlight in this simplified model.

For this model, the same ε is used for atmospheric infrared emissivity. A larger ε represents a more infrared-absorbing atmosphere; it is not a literal greenhouse-gas concentration.

Tₛ = [F / (σ(1 − ε/2))]1/4

Why does outgoing infrared return to the same value at equilibrium?

This is a general consequence of energy conservation, not just a result of the single-layer model. Earth's total energy changes according to the difference between absorbed sunlight and outgoing radiation to space:

Rate of energy storage per unit area = Fabsorbed − FIR,out

At equilibrium, the energy stored in the Earth–atmosphere system is no longer changing. If the solar constant S and planetary albedo α are fixed, absorbed sunlight is Fabsorbed = (1 − α)S/4, and the outgoing infrared flux must equal that amount.

Immediately after greenhouse gas absorption increases: With temperatures initially unchanged, more infrared is absorbed and re-emitted by the atmosphere and less energy escapes to space. Thus FIR,out < Fabsorbed. The Earth–atmosphere system gains energy and begins to warm.

As the system warms: Warmer surface and atmospheric layers emit more infrared radiation. If a new equilibrium is reached, outgoing infrared again equals absorbed sunlight, but the surface is warmer than before.

At equilibrium: FIR,out = (1 − α)S/4 = (1 − ε)σTs⁴ + εσTa⁴

Albedo is different: Increasing α reflects more sunlight, decreases absorbed solar energy, and therefore decreases the outgoing infrared flux required at the new equilibrium.

Try it: Keep S and α fixed and increase ε. The sliders display the new equilibrium, not the brief period of imbalance: surface temperature increases while equilibrium outgoing IR remains unchanged. Next increase α and observe that both absorbed sunlight and equilibrium outgoing IR decrease.

Model limitation: This interactive model jumps directly from one equilibrium state to another. It does not simulate the time-dependent warming process. Real-world greenhouse-gas changes can also alter clouds, ice, and albedo, which may change the absorbed sunlight.

F = (1 − α)S/4 is the globally averaged absorbed sunlight, shared with the zeroth-order tab. The idealized atmosphere emits infrared both upward and downward. This single-layer radiative equilibrium model omits convection, evaporation, clouds, and vertical temperature structure. When ε = 0, the atmospheric temperature is not defined by the radiation equations and is displayed as N/A.
