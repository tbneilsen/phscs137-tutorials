Energy and Power: A Water-Pipe Analogy

PHSCS 137 · Interactive exploration · About 15–20 minutes

1. Understand the analogy

Imagine a tank supplying water through a main pipe that divides into branches. Each branch serves a different device. Opening a valve is like plugging in and turning on a device.

Water flow rate (L/min) represents electrical power (W). Water stored in the tank (L) represents battery energy (Wh). The main pipe's maximum flow capacity represents the power station's maximum output rating.

This is a conceptual analogy, not a detailed hydraulic or electrical circuit model. We assume each open branch draws a fixed flow rate, provided the source can supply the total.

Notice: The source is capable of supplying up to 350 L/min, but it supplies only the amount requested by open branches. Likewise, a 350 W power station does not force 350 W into a 50 W laptop.

4. Connect the water model to electricity

The same branch choices represent appliances plugged into a 296 Wh battery with a maximum output of 350 W. When a device is on, its listed wattage is the power it draws in this simplified model.

Total power drawn: add the wattages of all connected devices. Ideal battery operating time: divide 296 Wh by that total power in W. If the total exceeds 350 W, the power station cannot support the combination at its rated power.

Real appliances may draw varying power. Actual battery life can be shorter because of conversion losses, battery limits, and other factors.
